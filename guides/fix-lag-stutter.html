How to Fix Lag and Stutter in PUBG on PC

PUBG PC Guide · Updated November 2026 · 6 min read

Nothing ruins a PUBG match faster than lag and stutter: your aim jumps, enemies teleport, and the game freezes at the worst moment. The good news is that most lag has a clear cause and a clear fix.

First, Find Out Which Kind of Lag You Have

Lag is not one problem, and the fix depends on which type you have. Network lag means high ping, rubber-banding, and teleporting enemies. Frame-rate lag means choppy movement, low FPS, and stuttering when you turn or land in a busy town. Tell them apart by watching the numbers: turn on network statistics in the game settings to see your ping, and use a frame-rate display to see your FPS. High ping with fine FPS means a connection problem; low FPS with fine ping means a PC or settings problem. Fixing the wrong one wastes your time, so spend two minutes diagnosing before you change anything.

Fix Your Connection and Lower Your Ping

For network lag, a wired connection is the single biggest improvement. Wi-Fi adds delay and is easily disturbed by walls and other devices, while an ethernet cable gives you a stable, low-latency link to your router. Next, pick the closest server region: a server on the other side of the world adds unavoidable delay no setting can remove. Close downloads, cloud backups, and video streams while you play: they compete for the same connection. If others at home are streaming video, ask them to pause, or use your router's quality-of-service settings to prioritise your PC. Restart your router once a week too: routers build up stale connections over time. If your ping is still high on a wired connection to the nearest server, your internet plan may be the problem, and a faster plan is the only real fix.

Fix Low FPS and Stuttering

When your frame rate is the problem, start with the in-game settings. Shadows, effects, and post-processing cost the most performance for the least visual benefit, so set them to low. Textures can stay on medium with at least 6 GB of graphics memory. Turn off motion blur and depth of field: they cost frames and hide enemies. Next, update your graphics drivers in the Nvidia app or AMD Software: new drivers often include performance fixes for PUBG. Then check what else is running: close your browser, especially with many tabs open, and any recording or streaming software you are not using. PUBG needs plenty of RAM, so with only 8 GB, background apps steal memory and cause stutter every time the game loads a new part of the map.

Stop the Micro-Stutters When Landing and Fighting

The most annoying stutter is the freeze when you land or drive into a new town, usually your storage or memory struggling to load the map. If PUBG is on an old hard drive, move it to an SSD: in Steam, right-click the game, open Properties, then Installed Files, and choose "Move install folder". The difference is dramatic. If you already use an SSD, check it is not almost full, because a nearly full drive slows down badly. Keep at least 15 percent free. Another common cause is Windows power settings: in the Control Panel, open Power Options and choose the High Performance plan, because the Balanced plan can throttle your CPU at exactly the wrong moment. Finally, verify your game files in Steam to repair any damaged files, which can also cause random hitches.

Quick test: after each fix, play one full match before trying the next one. If you change five things at once and the lag disappears, you will never know which fix worked, or what to do when it comes back.

Keep Your PC Cool and Clean

Heat causes stutter too: when your CPU or graphics card gets too hot, it slows down to avoid damage, and that slowdown shows up as a frame-rate drop mid-fight. This is especially common on laptops and desktops that have not been cleaned in a while. Check your temperatures with a free tool such as HWMonitor while you play: a CPU under 85 degrees and a GPU under 80 are fine, but higher means your cooling needs attention. Clean the dust from your fans and vents with compressed air, and make sure your case fans spin and point the right way: cool air in at the front, hot air out at the back. On a laptop, raise the back edge slightly so air flows underneath, and use a cooling pad for long sessions. A cooler PC holds its frame rate steady instead of dropping at the worst moment.

When Nothing Works: A Short Checklist

If PUBG still lags after everything above, work through this short list. First, restart your PC: a fresh boot clears memory leaks and stuck background processes. Second, install any Windows updates and restart again. Third, run a full malware scan, because unwanted software can eat your CPU and bandwidth in the background. Fourth, try a different server region for one match to rule out a problem with your usual server. Fifth, check whether other games lag too: if they do, the fault is your PC or connection; if only PUBG lags, it may be a game update or server issue, and waiting for a patch is the honest answer. Most players find their fix in the first three sections, so do not give up after one try.

Frequently Asked Questions

Why does PUBG stutter when I land?

Landing stutter is usually caused by the map loading slowly. Install PUBG on an SSD instead of a hard drive, keep at least 15 percent of the drive free, and close background apps so the game has enough RAM. This fixes the problem for most players.

What ping is good for PUBG on PC?

Under 50 ms is excellent, 50 to 100 ms is playable, and anything above 150 ms will feel laggy, with enemies teleporting and shots registering late. Use a wired connection and the closest server region to keep your ping as low as possible.

Does lowering graphics settings fix lag?

It fixes low FPS and frame stutter, but not network lag. If your ping is high, changing graphics settings will not help, because the problem is your connection. Lower shadows, effects, and post-processing first, as those cost the most performance.
