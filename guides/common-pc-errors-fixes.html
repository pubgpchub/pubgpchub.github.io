Common PUBG PC Errors and How to Fix Them

PUBG PC Guide · Updated November 2026 · 5 min read

Nothing is more frustrating than PUBG crashing, refusing to launch, or disconnecting mid-match. This guide covers the most common PUBG errors on PC and the safe, step-by-step fixes that solve most of them.

Before You Start: The Quick Checks

Most PUBG problems have a simple cause, so start with the basics before you try anything advanced. Restart your PC, close unnecessary programs, and check that your internet connection is stable. Make sure Windows and your graphics drivers are up to date, since old drivers are a common cause of crashes. Check that your PC meets the minimum system requirements and that you have enough free disk space. In Steam, right-click PUBG, choose Properties, then Installed Files, and click "Verify integrity of game files". This checks your game files and repairs any that are missing or damaged. If the problem started after a recent update, check the official PUBG support pages to see if other players are reporting the same issue.

Game Will Not Launch or Crashes on Startup

If PUBG will not launch at all, or crashes before the main menu, try running Steam as an administrator and then launching the game from your library. If that does not help, verify the game files again and clear the Steam download cache from Steam settings. Outdated or corrupted graphics drivers are another common cause: download the latest driver directly from NVIDIA or AMD, choose a clean installation if offered, and restart your PC. Overlay software can also interfere, so try disabling the Steam overlay, Discord overlay, and any screen-recording software temporarily. If the game crashes with a message about a missing file or a Visual C++ component, reinstall the Visual C++ redistributables from Microsoft and make sure DirectX is up to date through Windows Update. As a last step, uninstall PUBG, restart your PC, and reinstall it on a drive with plenty of free space.

Crashes, Freezes and Low FPS During Matches

Crashes during a match are often linked to overheating, unstable overclocks, or graphics settings that are too high for your hardware. Check your CPU and GPU temperatures; if they are very high, clean dust from your fans, improve airflow, and lower your in-game settings. Turn off any CPU or GPU overclock temporarily to see if the crashes stop, since even a small unstable overclock can crash a demanding game. Lower the most demanding settings first: shadows, effects, post-processing, and anti-aliasing. If the game freezes briefly and then recovers, it may be loading assets from a slow hard drive; moving PUBG to an SSD usually fixes this. Close background apps, especially browsers with many tabs. If crashes happen only on one map, verify the game files again.

Connection, Lag and Disconnection Errors

Connection errors in PUBG usually show as high ping, rubber-banding, or being kicked back to the lobby. Use a wired Ethernet connection instead of Wi-Fi when possible, because it is more stable and has lower latency. Restart your router and modem, and avoid downloading large files or streaming video on the same connection while you play. In PUBG, choose the server region closest to you for the lowest ping. If you see a "network lag detected" message, check whether other devices on your network are using bandwidth, and test your connection speed to confirm your upload and download rates. A VPN can sometimes help if your route to the game server is poor, but it can also make ping worse, so test it carefully and do not use it as a default fix. If disconnections happen at the same time every day, your internet provider may be doing maintenance or throttling traffic, and a call to them may be needed. Never use third-party tools that claim to fix lag by changing game files; they can get your account banned.

When to Contact PUBG Support

If you have tried the fixes above and the problem continues, contact official PUBG support. Before you do, gather the details they will ask for: your Steam ID, the exact error message, when the problem started, what you were doing when it happened, and what you have already tried. Take a screenshot of any error message and note your PC specifications, including your graphics card, processor, memory, and Windows version. Submit your report through the official PUBG support website, not through random forums or third-party sites that ask for your login details. Never share your Steam password, and never download a "fix" tool from an unofficial site, since these are often malware. Support may ask for game logs from the PUBG installation folder.

Important: Only download drivers and fixes from official sources: Steam, Microsoft, NVIDIA, AMD, and the official PUBG support site. Unofficial "fix" programs and modified game files can contain malware and can lead to a game ban.

Frequently Asked Questions

Why does PUBG keep crashing on my PC?

The most common causes are outdated graphics drivers, overheating, an unstable overclock, and corrupt game files. Update your drivers, check your temperatures, remove any overclock, and verify the game files in Steam.

How do I verify PUBG game files on Steam?

Right-click PUBG in your Steam library, choose Properties, then Installed Files, and click "Verify integrity of game files". Steam will check every file and download replacements for any that are missing or damaged.

Will reinstalling PUBG delete my progress?

No. Your account progress, skins, and stats are stored on PUBG servers and linked to your Steam account, not on your PC. Reinstalling the game will not delete them, though you will need to download the game again.
