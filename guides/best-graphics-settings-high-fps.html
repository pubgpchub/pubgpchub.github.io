Best PUBG Graphics Settings for High FPS on PC

PUBG PC Guide · Updated November 2026 · 5 min read

In PUBG, frames win fights. A higher, steadier frame rate means smoother aiming, faster reactions and less stutter when an enemy appears around a corner. You do not need a new graphics card to get there: the right settings can add 30 to 50 percent more frames on most PCs. Here is the setup we recommend, with the reasoning behind every choice.

Why Settings Matter More Than Raw Power

PUBG renders huge maps with long sightlines, dense grass, detailed buildings and up to 100 players. On the default High or Ultra presets, your graphics card spends most of its effort on things that do not help you win, such as fancy shadows and post-processing effects. Turning those down frees up power for what does matter: a high, stable frame rate and clear visibility. Competitive players have known this for years, which is why almost all of them play on Low and Medium settings even on powerful hardware. The goal is not the prettiest picture; it is the smoothest, clearest one.

The High-FPS Settings at a Glance

Open Settings, then Graphics, and apply these values as a starting point. They are tuned for a typical mid-range PC at 1080p. If your hardware is stronger, raise textures and view distance; if it is weaker, drop the resolution scale first.

Settings to Turn Down First

If your frame rate is still low after applying the table above, work through this order, because these settings cost the most performance for the least benefit. Shadows come first: on Ultra they can cost 20 frames per second or more, and they rarely help you spot anyone. Post-processing is next, since it adds blur, bloom and depth effects that actively make the game harder to read. Foliage follows, because thick grass is both expensive to render and a hiding place for enemies. Effects are fourth, since smoke and explosions look impressive but fill your screen at the worst moments. Render scale is the last resort: dropping it to 90 or 80 will raise your frame rate, but the whole image gets softer, so only use it if nothing else works.

Settings That Are Safe to Keep High

Not everything needs to be on Low. Textures are the best example: once they are loaded into your graphics card memory, higher textures cost almost nothing in frame rate, and they make enemies and buildings look crisper. View distance is worth keeping on Medium or High too, because it controls how far away objects and players render. Dropping it too low can make distant enemies pop in late or not at all, which is a real competitive disadvantage. Anti-aliasing on Medium is also a good trade: it cleans up jagged edges on rooftops and fences without the heavy cost of the higher options. Keep these three reasonably high and spend your savings on the settings listed above.

Important: Always play in Fullscreen, not Borderless or Windowed. Fullscreen gives the game exclusive access to your graphics card, which lowers input lag and usually adds a few frames. Borderless is convenient for alt-tabbing, but it costs performance and adds delay to your mouse.

Frame Rate Cap, Smoothing and Monitor Settings

Once your settings are right, cap your frame rate slightly above your monitor refresh rate, for example 144 on a 144 Hz monitor, so the game does not waste power rendering frames you cannot see. Turn off frame rate smoothing if your PC is strong enough to hold a steady rate, since smoothing can add input lag; turn it on only if your frame rate swings wildly. Make sure your monitor is actually running at its highest refresh rate in Windows display settings, because many monitors ship set to 60 Hz by default. Finally, enable your graphics card's low-latency mode in its control panel if the option exists, and update your drivers, since new driver releases often include PUBG performance improvements.

Testing and Fine-Tuning Your Setup

Do not judge your settings from the lobby. Load into the Training Mode, enable the in-game FPS counter in Steam settings or your graphics card overlay, and run through a busy area with smoke grenades and vehicles. Then play two or three real matches and watch the frame rate during the parachute drop and in the final circles, which are the hardest moments for your PC. If your frame rate stays above your monitor's refresh rate most of the time, you are done. If it dips, lower shadows or effects one step and test again. Change one setting at a time so you know what actually helped, and save your final setup with a screenshot so you can restore it after a game update resets your options.

Frequently Asked Questions

What is the best overall graphics preset for PUBG?

There is no single best preset, but a custom mix of Low and Medium settings beats every built-in preset for frame rate. Use the table in this guide as your starting point and adjust from there.

Does lowering render scale increase FPS?

Yes, lowering render scale below 100 raises your frame rate noticeably, but it makes the whole image blurrier and harder to read at distance. Try it only after turning down shadows, post-processing and foliage.

Should I use V-Sync in PUBG?

No, keep V-Sync off in most cases. It can cause input lag and stuttering in a fast game like PUBG. Use your monitor's adaptive sync instead if your hardware supports it.
