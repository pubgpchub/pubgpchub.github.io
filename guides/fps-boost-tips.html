PUBG FPS Boost: 15 Tips to Raise Frame Rates on PC

PUBG PC Guide · Updated November 2026 · 6 min read

Low frame rates make PUBG feel sluggish and cost you fights you should have won. The good news is that most performance problems come from settings and background clutter, not from weak hardware, and they are free to fix. These 15 tips are ordered from the biggest gains to the smallest, so work through them in order and test your frame rate after each group.

Tips 1 to 4: In-Game Settings That Cost You Frames

  1. Set shadows to Very Low. Shadows are the single most expensive setting in PUBG and can cost 20 frames per second or more on Ultra. Very Low looks flat, but it is the biggest free gain available.
  2. Turn post-processing to Very Low. This removes blur, bloom and haze effects that eat performance and make distant enemies harder to see.
  3. Drop foliage to Very Low. Thick grass is expensive to render and hides enemies. Thin grass costs less and improves visibility at the same time.
  4. Play in Fullscreen, not Borderless. Fullscreen gives the game exclusive access to your graphics card, which lowers input lag and usually adds a few frames per second.

These four changes alone can lift a struggling PC from 40 frames per second to 60 or more. If your frame rate is still low after them, lower effects and anti-aliasing next, and only reduce render scale below 100 as a last resort, since it blurs the whole image.

Tips 5 to 8: Windows Settings Worth Changing

  1. Turn on Windows Game Mode. Open Settings, then Gaming, then Game Mode, and switch it on. It tells Windows to prioritize the game over background tasks while you play.
  2. Set the power plan to High Performance. In Windows power settings, choose High Performance so your processor and graphics card are not throttled to save energy.
  3. Disable fullscreen optimizations. Right-click the PUBG executable, open Properties, then Compatibility, and tick "Disable fullscreen optimizations". This fixes stuttering for some players.
  4. Turn off background recording. Windows Game Bar recording and similar capture tools use CPU and disk while you play. Turn them off unless you are actively recording, and use your graphics card's own recorder instead if you need one.

Tips 9 to 11: Drivers, Updates and Game Files

  1. Update your graphics drivers. New driver releases regularly include performance fixes for popular games. Download them from NVIDIA or AMD directly, not from a third-party updater.
  2. Keep PUBG and Windows updated. Game patches often include optimization fixes, and Windows updates can fix performance bugs. Check for updates before blaming your hardware.
  3. Verify the game files. In Steam, right-click PUBG, choose Properties, then Installed Files, then Verify integrity of game files. Corrupted files can cause stuttering and crashes that look like a performance problem.

Quick check: Before changing anything else, open Task Manager while the game is running and look at the Performance tab. If your CPU is at 100 percent while your GPU sits at 50, you are CPU-limited and settings will help less than closing background programs. If the GPU is maxed out, graphics settings are your best lever.

Tips 12 and 13: Free Up System Resources

  1. Close your browser and heavy apps before playing. Chrome with many tabs open can use several gigabytes of RAM. Close it, along with Discord overlays you do not need, music apps and cloud sync tools, before launching PUBG.
  2. Install PUBG on an SSD. An SSD will not raise your frame rate, but it cuts loading times and stops the texture pop-in and hitching that hard-drive players see after landing. If your SSD is nearly full, free up space first.

Tips 14 and 15: Hardware Upgrades That Matter Most

  1. Upgrade from 8 GB to 16 GB of RAM. This is the cheapest meaningful upgrade for most players. With 8 GB, Windows and PUBG compete for memory, which causes stuttering when you land or drive across the map.
  2. Upgrade the graphics card before the processor. If you can only afford one upgrade, the GPU gives the biggest frame rate gain in PUBG for most systems. A mid-range card such as an RTX 3060 or RX 6600 will run the game smoothly at 1080p on a mix of Medium and High settings.

If you have worked through all 15 tips and your frame rate is still below 60, your hardware is simply below what the game needs, and no further setting will fix that. At that point, a single targeted upgrade is a better investment than more tweaking.

Frequently Asked Questions

What is the fastest way to boost FPS in PUBG?

Set shadows, post-processing and foliage to Very Low and play in Fullscreen. Those four changes give the biggest immediate gain on almost any PC.

Do FPS boost programs actually work?

Most "FPS booster" apps do little more than close background programs, which you can do yourself for free. Stick to the settings and steps in this guide and avoid programs that promise unrealistic gains.

Will an SSD increase my FPS in PUBG?

No, an SSD does not raise frame rates, but it does cut loading times and reduce stuttering and blurry textures after landing. It is worth it, just not as an FPS fix.
