PUBG Training Mode Drills: Aim, Recoil & Movement Practice

PUBG PC Guide · Updated November 2026 · 6 min read

Most PUBG players improve slowly because they only practise during real matches, where a mistake costs you the whole game. Training Mode removes that cost: you can repeat the same shot a hundred times, test every weapon, and drill movement until it is automatic. This guide gives you a structured routine that builds match-ready skill.

How Training Mode Works

Training Mode is a dedicated map with shooting ranges, a driving track, a parachute area and zones for grenades and vehicles. Pick up any weapon and attachment from the tables, set target distances, and reset as often as you like. Nothing here affects your rank or stats, so it is the perfect place to experiment.

Use the exact mouse sensitivity, keybinds and graphics settings you play matches with, because muscle memory built on different settings does not transfer. Warm up for five minutes, then move on to focused drills. A short daily session beats one long weekend session, because aim improves through regular repetition.

Aim Drills: Flicks and Tracking

Start at the shooting range with an assault rifle and a red dot sight. Pick a target at 50 metres and flick to it from a neutral position, firing one or two controlled shots, then returning to centre. Do ten repetitions, then switch to a different distance. The goal is not speed at first, but accuracy: a slow accurate flick becomes fast with practice, while a fast sloppy flick stays sloppy.

Next, practise tracking: strafe sideways while keeping your crosshair on a fixed target. Smooth tracking wins mid-range fights, where enemies move between cover. Spend five minutes on flicks and five on tracking each session, and change the target distance every few days so you are comfortable at every range.

Recoil Control Drills

Every weapon has a distinct recoil pattern, and learning it is the fastest way to improve your damage output. Pick one weapon at a time, for example the M416, and spray a full magazine at a wall from 30 metres. Look at the pattern, then repeat while pulling the mouse down smoothly to counter it. Aim for a tight group, not a vertical line climbing the wall.

Once you can control a spray standing still, add movement: strafe left, stop, spray a short burst, then strafe right. In real fights you rarely stand still, so your recoil control must work while moving. Practise with the weapons you actually use rather than every gun in the game, and revisit each briefly every week so the patterns stay fresh.

Movement and Peeking Drills

Good movement wins fights that good aim alone cannot. Use the cover near the range to practise peeking: lean out, take a shot, and return to cover before the enemy can react. Practise left and right leans, crouch peeks, and jump peeks over low walls. Expose yourself for the shortest possible time and always return to the same safe position.

Also practise counter-strafing: tap the opposite movement key to stop instantly before shooting. Moving while firing spreads your bullets, so stopping cleanly before each burst makes your shots far more accurate. Drill this until it feels natural: strafe, stop, shoot a short burst, strafe the other way, and repeat.

Routine: A solid daily warm-up is 30 minutes: 5 minutes of flicks, 5 of tracking, 10 of recoil control with your main weapon, 5 of peeking, and 5 of spray transfer. Then play matches while your hands are warm.

Scope, Spray Transfer and Utility Practice

Spray transfer is moving your fire from one target to another without stopping. Set up two targets a few metres apart and knock the first down, then snap to the second in the same magazine. This wins squad fights, where you often face two enemies at once. Start slowly and build speed only when your accuracy holds.

Use the grenade area to practise throw distances and cooking times for frags, smokes and molotovs. Learn how far a frag flies with a short cook versus a long one, and practise throwing smokes over walls to cover a revive or rotation. Utility wins fights quietly: a well-placed smoke is often worth more than a good spray.

Turning Practice Into Match Skill

Training Mode only helps if you connect it to real matches. After each session, pick one thing to focus on in your next few games, such as counter-strafing before every burst or using smokes on rotations. Focus on one skill at a time; trying to fix everything at once fixes nothing.

Track progress loosely rather than obsessing over numbers. If your sprays feel tighter, your flicks land more often, and you win more close fights than last month, the drills are working. Keep a simple routine and practise a little every day: aim and movement improve over weeks, not overnight, but the gains are permanent.

Frequently Asked Questions

How long should I spend in Training Mode each day?

Twenty to thirty minutes is enough for a focused warm-up and drill session. Longer sessions have diminishing returns, and your time is usually better spent playing real matches after your hands are warm.

Which weapon should I practise with first?

Start with your main assault rifle, usually the M416 or AKM, since it is the weapon you use most. Once its recoil feels controlled, add your secondary weapon and any scoped rifle you use regularly.

Does Training Mode improve my rank?

Not directly, since nothing in Training Mode affects your stats or rank. But the aim, recoil control and movement you build there transfer directly into ranked matches, which is where your rank actually climbs.
