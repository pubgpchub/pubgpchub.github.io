Best Keyboard Settings and Keybinds for PUBG on PC

PUBG PC Guide · Updated November 2026 · 6 min read

In PUBG, the half-second it takes to heal, swap weapons, or lean around a corner often decides the fight. Smart keybinds put every important action within easy reach of your left hand, so you react without thinking.

Why Keybinds Matter More Than You Think

Every action in PUBG has a default key, but the defaults were not designed for speed. Healing with the number keys, for example, forces your fingers to travel across the keyboard and opens the inventory in the middle of a fight. Players who rebind heal faster, switch weapons instantly, and peek corners without breaking movement. The improvement is not about skill; it is about removing delays. If two players spot each other at the same time, the one whose fingers already know where every key is will usually fire first and win. Spending twenty minutes setting up good keybinds is one of the highest-value changes a new PC player can make.

The Most Important Keybinds to Change

Start with the actions you use in every fight. Healing is the biggest one: bind bandages, first aid kits, and boosters to keys your fingers can reach without looking. The number keys are often too far, so rebinding heals to F, G, or mouse side buttons works well. Keep 1 and 2 for your primary weapons, and make sure the swap is comfortable. Leaning with Q and E is already well placed, and you should use it constantly: peeking around a tree or window while only exposing your head makes you a much smaller target. Crouch on C or Left Ctrl is personal preference, but C keeps your pinky free for Shift. Finally, bind the map to a fast key such as M, because checking the zone should never be a chore.

Mouse Buttons: Use Them Wisely

If your mouse has side buttons, they are some of the most valuable keys you own. Your thumb rests on them already, so actions bound there cost you nothing. Good choices are healing items, switching to your pistol, or toggling scope zoom. A common setup is to put bandages on one side button and first aid kits on the other, so healing becomes a single thumb press instead of a keyboard reach. Avoid binding anything you might press by accident, such as the map or inventory, because a mistimed press can get you killed. Test any new mouse bind in the training mode first, so you know exactly what it does before you rely on it in a real match.

Tip: change one or two keybinds at a time and play at least five matches before changing more. Rebinding everything at once feels fast in the settings menu, but in a real fight your fingers will freeze while they search for keys they have not learned yet.

Movement Keys and Advanced Techniques

Good movement starts with the basics: hold Shift to sprint, tap C to crouch-slide down hills, and use Space to jump over low walls and fences. Two movement habits separate experienced players from beginners. The first is jump-peeking: jumping briefly over a wall to spot an enemy, then dropping back into cover before they can react. The second is crouch-spamming in close fights, which makes your head harder to track. Both depend on comfortable keys, so if jumping or crouching feels awkward, move those binds closer to your fingers. Also consider auto-run, which moves you forward without holding W during long rotations. Finally, practise switching between walking and sprinting quietly: walking makes far less noise, and enemies who hear your footsteps will know exactly where you are.

How to Practise Your New Keybinds

New keybinds feel slow at first, and that is normal. The fastest way to learn them is to spend ten minutes in the training mode doing a simple loop: sprint to a target, lean and shoot, swap weapons, heal, and repeat. Do this until the keys feel automatic, then play normal matches and accept that you will be a little slower for a few days. Do not switch back to your old binds after one bad game. Muscle memory takes about a week of regular play to rebuild, and after that the new layout will feel like it was always yours. Screenshot your final layout, so you can restore it quickly if an update ever resets your settings.

Frequently Asked Questions

What are the best keybinds for healing in PUBG?

Bind bandages, first aid kits, and boosters to keys near WASD or to your mouse side buttons. Keys like F, G, and H, or the mouse thumb buttons, let you heal with one press instead of opening the inventory. The exact keys matter less than keeping them close and practising until they are automatic.

Should I change the default keybinds in PUBG?

Yes, at least for healing and leaning. The defaults work, but they are slow in fights. Rebinding heals to nearby keys and using Q and E to lean will make you noticeably faster. Change a few keys at a time rather than everything at once.

How long does it take to learn new keybinds?

About a week of regular play. The first few matches will feel awkward, and you may press the wrong key under pressure. Practise in the training mode first, then keep the same layout for at least ten matches before judging it. Switching layouts constantly is what slows learning down.
