Laptop vs Desktop for PUBG: Which Is Better for PC Players?

PUBG PC Guide · Updated November 2026 · 6 min read

Both laptops and desktops can run PUBG well in 2026, but they win in different ways: a desktop gives you more performance for your money, a laptop gives you the freedom to play anywhere. The right choice depends on how and where you play.

Performance: Desktop Wins at the Same Price

At the same price, a desktop almost always delivers more frames per second in PUBG. Desktop parts run at higher power and have room for big coolers, so a desktop graphics card with the same name as a laptop one is usually faster in practice. A laptop RTX 4060, for example, is a cut-down version that runs slower than a desktop RTX 4060, and the gap grows in long sessions as heat builds. Laptops also share a small chassis between the CPU and GPU, so when both get hot, the system slows them down to protect itself. That said, a good gaming laptop with an RTX 4060 or better still runs PUBG at 80 to 120 FPS on competitive settings, which is perfectly playable. The desktop advantage is real, but it is a gap in frame rates, not between playable and unplayable.

Price and Value for Money

Desktops are cheaper to buy and cheaper to own. You pay for the parts, not for a screen, keyboard, battery, and a slim chassis, so a $1,000 desktop typically outperforms a $1,000 laptop by a clear margin. Desktops are also cheaper to repair and upgrade: a dead fan is a $15 part, a slow graphics card can be swapped in an afternoon, and you can add memory or storage any time. With a laptop, most parts are soldered or hard to reach, so an upgrade often means buying a whole new machine. On the other hand, a laptop includes everything in one price, while a desktop needs a monitor, keyboard, and mouse on top of the tower. A decent 144 Hz monitor alone can add $150 or more, narrowing the gap for a first-time buyer.

Cooling, Noise, and Long Sessions

Cooling is the hidden difference between laptops and desktops. PUBG sessions often last hours, and heat is the enemy of steady performance. A desktop with a mesh case and a few fans keeps its parts cool with little noise, so frame rates stay stable from the first match to the last. A gaming laptop has to push hot air through tiny vents, so its fans spin fast and loud, and the keyboard area can get uncomfortably warm. After a year or two of heavy use, dust builds up inside a laptop more quickly, and performance can drop until you clean it, which is harder than cleaning a desktop. If you play long sessions every day, the desktop's cooling is a genuine advantage. For a laptop, a cooling pad and a raised rear edge help airflow, but only partly close the gap.

Screen, Keyboard, and Comfort

A desktop lets you choose your screen, and for PUBG that matters: a 24 or 27-inch 144 Hz monitor shows enemies more clearly than a 15-inch laptop screen, and you can sit at a comfortable distance with a proper mouse and keyboard. A laptop screen is smaller and fixed, and most laptop keyboards are cramped for long gaming sessions, with less key travel and no room for a large mouse pad. Many laptop players solve this by plugging in an external monitor, keyboard, and mouse at home, turning the laptop into a desktop on the desk while keeping it portable elsewhere. That setup works well, but it adds cost, and if you are buying all those accessories anyway, a desktop looks like the simpler choice.

The honest answer: if you play in one place, buy a desktop. If you travel, move between rooms, study away from home, or share a small space, buy a laptop. Performance matters, but only if you are actually at your desk to enjoy it.

Which Should You Buy?

Choose a desktop if you play mostly at home, want the highest frame rates for your budget, and like upgrading parts over time. Choose a laptop if you need to carry your PC, play in different places, or want one machine that handles gaming, study, and work. If you go the laptop route, look for at least an RTX 4060 graphics card, 16 GB of RAM, a 144 Hz screen, and a model with good reviews for cooling, because a laptop that overheats will throttle no matter how strong its parts look on paper. If you go the desktop route, put the money you save toward a 144 Hz monitor and a comfortable mouse. Either way, both machines run PUBG well in 2026, and the best choice is the one that fits your life, not just the one with the biggest numbers on the box.

Frequently Asked Questions

Can a gaming laptop run PUBG smoothly?

Yes. A gaming laptop with an RTX 4060 or better, 16 GB of RAM, and an SSD runs PUBG at 80 to 120 FPS on competitive settings. It will not match a desktop at the same price, and it will run hotter and louder, but the game is fully playable and competitive on a good laptop.

Is a desktop better than a laptop for PUBG?

For raw performance, yes. A desktop gives you more FPS per dollar, better cooling, and easy upgrades. A laptop wins on portability. If you play in one place, the desktop is the better buy. If you need to move around, the laptop is worth the performance trade-off.

Can I upgrade a gaming laptop for PUBG later?

Usually only RAM and storage. The CPU and GPU in most gaming laptops are soldered to the motherboard and cannot be replaced. On a desktop, you can upgrade the graphics card, processor, memory, and storage independently, which makes it much cheaper to keep up with new games over time.
