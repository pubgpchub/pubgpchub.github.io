Best Monitor Settings for PUBG: Refresh Rate, Response Time & Color

PUBG PC Guide · Updated November 2026 · 6 min read

Your monitor is the last link between the game and your eyes, and in PUBG it can be the difference between spotting an enemy first and being spotted. A well-tuned monitor makes distant movement clearer, reduces blur when you flick, and keeps dark buildings readable. This guide covers the settings that matter most.

Refresh Rate: The Biggest Upgrade

Refresh rate, measured in hertz, is how many times per second your monitor redraws the image. A 60 Hz monitor shows 60 frames per second at most, while a 144 Hz monitor shows up to 144. In a gunfight, the higher rate makes enemy movement smoother and lets you track targets with less blur, which directly helps your aim.

For PUBG, 144 Hz is the sweet spot for most players, and 165 Hz or 240 Hz is better still if your PC can push the frames to match. A high refresh rate monitor only helps when your frame rate is high too, so set your monitor to its maximum refresh rate in Windows display settings, then check PUBG is actually running above 144 FPS. If your PC cannot sustain it, a 144 Hz monitor at 120 FPS still feels far better than 60 Hz.

Response Time and Overdrive

Response time is how quickly a pixel changes color, and slow pixels leave ghosting trails behind moving objects. Look for a monitor with a 1 ms response time, and set its overdrive or response-time option to a medium level in the on-screen menu. Medium overdrive usually gives the cleanest motion; the fastest setting often causes inverse ghosting, bright halos around moving objects.

Test your setting in Training Mode by strafing past a wall and watching for smearing. If moving objects leave a trail, raise the overdrive one step. If you see bright outlines around moving edges, lower it one step. Every panel is different, so trust your eyes rather than the marketing number on the box.

Brightness, Contrast and Black Levels

PUBG has many dark interiors, and a monitor that crushes shadows hides enemies crouched in corners. Start with brightness around 70 to 80 percent and contrast near 70, then adjust inside a building in the game. You want detail in dark corners without the image looking washed out. If your monitor has a black equalizer or shadow boost feature, a low setting can lift dark areas without blowing out bright skies.

Keep the black level setting matched to your connection: use full RGB range when connected over DisplayPort. A mismatched black level makes blacks look grey or crushes detail, and it is one of the most common reasons a monitor looks wrong out of the box.

Color Settings That Help You Spot Enemies

Many players increase digital vibrance in their graphics driver, often called saturation on AMD cards, to make enemy models stand out against grass and foliage. A modest increase, around 60 to 70 percent on NVIDIA or 130 to 150 on AMD, makes colors pop without turning the game into a cartoon. Avoid extreme values, which make distant camouflaged players harder to read.

For the monitor itself, choose the standard or FPS picture preset rather than a vivid movie mode, and set the color temperature to neutral or slightly cool. Warm presets add a yellow tint that dulls contrast in the game's green and brown environments.

Adaptive Sync and Recommended Settings

Adaptive sync, sold as FreeSync or G-Sync Compatible, matches the monitor's refresh rate to your frame rate and removes screen tearing without the input lag of traditional V-Sync. Turn it on in both the monitor menu and your graphics driver, and turn V-Sync off inside PUBG. Cap your frame rate a few frames below your monitor's maximum, for example 141 FPS on a 144 Hz display, to keep it active at all times.

Important: Always connect a high refresh rate monitor with DisplayPort, not HDMI, unless your monitor and cable both support the full refresh rate over HDMI. Many monitors are limited to 60 Hz over HDMI, and an expensive 144 Hz display will silently run at 60 Hz.

Panel Type and Resolution: What to Choose

For competitive PUBG, a fast IPS panel is the best all-round choice: it offers good colors, wide viewing angles and fast response times. TN panels are slightly faster but have washed-out colors and narrow viewing angles, while VA panels have deep blacks but can smear in fast motion. At 24 to 27 inches, 1080p is sharp enough for competitive play and easiest to drive at high frame rates, while 1440p looks better with a mid-range card or better.

Whatever you buy, spend ten minutes setting it up properly after unboxing. Use the settings above and test in a real match rather than the lobby. A correctly tuned mid-range monitor will serve you better than an expensive display left on its factory defaults.

Frequently Asked Questions

Is a 144 Hz monitor worth it for PUBG?

Yes, it is one of the most noticeable upgrades you can make. Movement looks smoother, tracking fast-moving enemies is easier, and the game feels more responsive, provided your PC can deliver frame rates above 100 FPS.

Should I use V-Sync in PUBG?

No. V-Sync adds input lag, which hurts your aim. Use adaptive sync with an FPS cap just below your refresh rate instead, which removes tearing without the delay.

Why does my 144 Hz monitor only show 60 Hz?

Check three things: connect with DisplayPort, set the refresh rate in Windows display settings, and confirm the setting in your graphics driver. Many monitors also need the high refresh rate enabled in their own on-screen menu.
